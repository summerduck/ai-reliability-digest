01 · arXiv cs.AI · 30 min read

Efficient Benchmarking in Production: A Study of an Evolving LLM Agent

Researchers studied how to cheaply re-benchmark a production analytics agent serving tens of thousands of users, comparing random sampling, historical caching, fixed subsets, and IRT-based adaptive testing across 574 historical runs. Multidimensional 2PL adaptive testing gave the best fidelity (1.03pp MAE at 38.5% of a full run), but the team deployed difficulty-stratified fixed subsets in production for operational simplicity, and found they transferred without recalibration to five other agent families.

Why it matters
This is essentially a blueprint for building a regression-test suite for a non-deterministic LLM agent that doesn't require rerunning the full benchmark on every change - directly applicable to designing CI eval gates for a conversational AI pipeline, and a great concrete talking point for interviews about test efficiency vs. fidelity tradeoffs.

Detecting Hallucination in LLMs: Tracing the Topological Signatures of Impaired Context Sharing

Researchers analyze attention graph topology (via Forman-Ricci curvature) to detect hallucinated responses in a single forward pass, finding hallucinations correlate with over-reliance on self-attention and information over-squashing in final layers. The method outperforms existing attention-based and multi-response baselines across two hallucination benchmarks.

Why it matters
